\begin{abstract}
This guide examines how a curated industrial cyber-physical systems dataset supports research in exposure analysis, knowledge graphs, grounded threat intelligence, behavioral monitoring, digital twins and governed AI agents. It distinguishes structural, synthetic, observational and expected-decision evidence, and identifies the additional labels, mappings and validation needed for stronger claims. Research questions are paired with suitable inputs, comparison designs, evaluation measures and limitations. The opening schematics establish the relationship between physical services, digital representations and analytical authority. Proposed experiments are distinguished from the release's recorded offline validation.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Context I: cyber-physical systems and critical infrastructure}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Context I: CPS and critical infrastructure}
A critical service relies on physical processes, control systems and organizational supervision. Cybersecurity failures can affect the integrity of observations, the availability of control or the correctness of decisions. An anomaly in a released record is not, by itself, evidence of a physical consequence. Figure~\ref{fig:cps} locates these dependencies within a representative CPS architecture [12].

\textbf{Dataset connection.} Inventory and dataflow tables describe selected structural relationships. Operational features describe pseudonymized event sequences. Synthetic logs support controlled comparisons. They support exposure, monitoring and evidence-interpretation studies; measured service loss requires additional process and incident evidence.

\textbf{Interpretation.} Commands and measurements are distinct information flows. A study should specify which flow is observed or perturbed, and what evidence links a deviation to an operational outcome. No live connection between this repository and physical equipment is asserted.

\section*{Context II: digital twins and governed agentic AI}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Context II: digital twins and agentic AI}
Digital twins represent selected entities, states and transitions for observation and analysis. Their usefulness depends on the credibility of the representation and its intended use [13]. An agent adds planning and tool selection, creating a separate question about permission to act. Recent agentic-AI guidance emphasizes bounded tasks, oversight and monitoring [14].

\textbf{Research implication.} Twin fidelity, answer correctness, policy agreement and operational safety are different constructs. The 400 expected agent decisions support fixture-level evaluation; the 29 event traces support bounded sequence research. Neither alone certifies the combined architecture.
